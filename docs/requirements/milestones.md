# Milestones

The milestone ladder for the product described in [product-requirements.md](product-requirements.md), built with the stack in [technical-requirements.md](technical-requirements.md). Each milestone is delivered as one Spec Kit feature.

The ladder follows a "walking skeleton" approach: first ship an empty skeleton to the internet, then grow it with vertical slices. Each step ends with a working, testable and meaningful result.

**Delivered milestones** are short summaries of what the product does after them. Their specification in [`specs/`](../../specs/) is the source of truth for details, and the summary is updated only if the product behaviour changes. **Upcoming milestones** hold the full scope, refined until every detail is clear, and are the input for `speckit-specify`.

## Delivered

1. **Hello World locally.** _Spec:_ [001-hello-world-page](../../specs/001-hello-world-page/spec.md)
   - A server-rendered home page that runs on a developer's machine, with a README for setup, running and testing.

2. **Deploy Hello World to the internet + CI/CD.** _Spec:_ [002-public-deploy-cicd](../../specs/002-public-deploy-cicd/spec.md)
   - The application is public over HTTPS. Pull requests are gated by tests and style checks, every merge to `main` is deployed automatically, and a status address reports health and version.

3. **Database + first entity.** _Spec:_ [003-database-questions](../../specs/003-database-questions/spec.md)
   - A persistent database with the first entity, the question (text and reference answer). The home page shows a database status line and stays up when the database is unreachable.

4. **Email code authentication.** _Spec:_ [004-email-otp-auth](../../specs/004-email-otp-auth/spec.md)
   - Passwordless sign-in with a one-time emailed code, only for users already known to the application, with rate limits, no account enumeration and 14-day sessions. Every page except sign-in, status and static files requires sign-in.

5. **Roles and authorization.** _Spec:_ [005-roles-authorization](../../specs/005-roles-authorization/spec.md)
   - Administrator, teacher and student roles. A person with several roles chooses one and can switch from the header; access follows the current role only and is denied by default.

6. **Administrator area and subjects.** _Spec:_ [006-admin-area](../../specs/006-admin-area/spec.md)
   - A tabbed administrator area. Administrators create, rename, deactivate, activate and delete (while unused) subjects.

7. **Administrator area: educational institutions.** _Spec:_ [007-region-and-institutions-management](../../specs/007-region-and-institutions-management/spec.md)
   - Administrators manage regions and the educational institutions in them with the same actions as subjects. Only an active institution in an active region can be selected.

8. **User management with consent: teachers.** _Spec:_ [008-teacher-management-consent](../../specs/008-teacher-management-consent/spec.md)
   - Administrators invite teachers by email; nothing is stored until the teacher accepts the versioned consent text. Teachers are linked to institutions with **Read** or **Write** access and have a competition-management setting. Every user has a profile with **Withdraw consent**, and **Delete my data** works without signing in.

9. **User management with consent: classes and students.** _Spec:_ [009-classes-students-management](../../specs/009-classes-students-management/spec.md)
   - A teacher area with a **Students** tab. Teachers with Write access manage classes and invite, edit, move and remove students through the same consent flow; Read access is view-only. Deactivating a region, institution or class blocks the students below it.

10. **Professional design system and UI guidelines.** _Spec:_ [010-design-system-ui-guidelines](../../specs/010-design-system-ui-guidelines/spec.md)
    - A visual identity, one shared page frame, a component library used by every page, and **Light** / **Dark** / **Device** themes. The binding UI guidelines are in [ui-guidelines.md](ui-guidelines.md), with a component gallery at `/admin/components` and an automated check.

10.1. **Feedback while a page loads, and a frame that stays in view.** _Spec:_ [010.1-loading-feedback-pinned-frame](../../specs/010.1-loading-feedback-pinned-frame/spec.md)
   - A loading bar for slow navigations, a busy state on the button that sent a form, and a top bar and area tabs pinned on tall screens. Everything works without JavaScript.

11. **Question bank (teacher).** _Spec:_ [011-question-bank](../../specs/011-question-bank/spec.md)
    - A **Questions** tab where every teacher browses the shared question bank by subject, paginated. Teachers with **Can manage competitions** add, edit and soft-delete questions.

11.1. **Teams (teacher).** _Spec:_ [011.1-teacher-teams](../../specs/011.1-teacher-teams/spec.md)
   - A **Teams** tab. Teachers with Write access create, rename and soft-delete teams of an institution and add and remove its students as members. Memberships are personal data, shown on the profile and erased with the student's data.

12.1. **Creating, editing and cancelling competitions (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.1-teacher-competitions](../../specs/012.1-teacher-competitions/spec.md)
   - A **Competitions** tab listing competitions by subject and status, paginated. Teachers with **Can manage competitions** add competitions, edit upcoming ones and cancel upcoming or in-progress ones. The status (**Upcoming**, **In progress**, **Finished**, **Cancelled**) is derived; times are stored in UTC and shown in the browser's time zone.

12.2. **Questions and teams of a competition (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.2-competition-questions-teams](../../specs/012.2-competition-questions-teams/spec.md)
   - Every competition row has an actions menu with **Questions** and **Teams**, which every teacher reads. Teachers with **Can manage competitions** add questions (copied from the bank when added) and teams of any institution, one region at a time, to an **Upcoming** competition, and remove them. Teams are listed in region boxes. Deleting a team takes it out of every competition that has not finished.

12.3. **Entering a team's answers (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.3-team-answers](../../specs/012.3-team-answers/spec.md)
   - On an **In progress** competition every teacher has **Answers**: their own institutions' teams in region boxes with each team's progress ("3 of 6"). With **Write** access a teacher enters a team's answers on one answer sheet, one text answer per question, saved at once; the last save wins. **Read** access shows only the progress (from milestone 12.4 it also opens the answers read-only). Deleting a team deletes its answers in every competition that has not finished.

12.4. **Finishing a competition and grading its answers by hand (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.4-finish-and-grade](../../specs/012.4-finish-and-grade/spec.md)
   - Teachers with **Can manage competitions** finish an **In progress** competition (**Finish competition**, a primary confirmation with **Keep in progress**); it is **Finished** for good and its answers can no longer be entered. On a **Finished** competition they open **Grades**: every team, whatever their links, with **Answered**, **Graded** and **Score**, an **Ungraded only** filter, and a grading sheet per team with the reference answers and one whole-number grade from 0 to 10 per answer, saved at once; the last save wins, and a team's score is the sum of its grades. On the **Competitions** tab **Read** access now opens a team's answers read-only; after finishing, the **Answers** pages are read-only for the teachers of a team's institution and show its grades and score.

## Upcoming

12. **Competitions (12.x).** A group of milestones, not a milestone of its own: it has no spec and no test. Together the 12.x milestones build the teacher's side of competitions: creating, editing and cancelling them, choosing their questions and teams, entering the teams' answers, grading the answers by hand, and stopping them. Milestone 12.5 is not about competitions: it makes the whole application bilingual, Ukrainian and English, before the student's side is built, so every later milestone is written in both languages from the start. Taking a competition (milestone 13), answer evaluation by an LLM (milestone 14) and results (milestone 15) come after.

12.5. **Ukrainian localisation.** The application is used in Ukrainian educational institutions, so it speaks Ukrainian: every page opens in Ukrainian, a language switch in the top bar changes it to English and back, Ukrainian names sort in the order of the Ukrainian alphabet, and the emails and the consent texts exist in Ukrainian. Ukrainian is the default language of the interface, of the consent texts and of every other communication with users. The stack gains one category, localisation: gettext catalogues managed with Babel on top of the existing Jinja2 templates (`jinja2.ext.i18n`); Babel is the only new dependency.

Participants come from schools and from colleges.
    - **UI guidelines:** [ui-guidelines.md](ui-guidelines.md) covers localisation: the top bar with the language switch (Layout); the `language_switch` component (Frame); times in both languages and the Kyiv-time fallback (Local time); the Ukrainian form of every wording pattern, the glossary and the Ukrainian writing rules below (Wording patterns); the switch and the top bar at phone width (Phone behaviour); how a message is written, extracted and translated, and that every page is checked in both languages (a new Languages section); and the catalogue checks (Checks). The theme switch stays where it is: in the user menu and under the sign-in form.
    _Interface and language switch:_ Everything on the screen is in the chosen language: every page, component, form error, notice, error page, page title, visually hidden text and `aria-label`, the status, role and access-level labels, counts and dates.
    - **The switch** is in the top bar of every page, signed in or signed out, including sign-in, invitation, data deletion and error pages, placed before the user menu. It looks like a combobox: it shows the current language's flag and name, and when opened lists both languages, each with its flag and its name written in that language: the Ukrainian flag with **Українська**, and the United Kingdom flag with **English**. It is a native `<details>` menu with a small form, like the user menu, so it works without JavaScript. The flags are small SVG images in `app/static/img/`, named by the country's code (ISO 3166-1: `flag-ua.svg` for Ukraine, `flag-gb.svg` for the United Kingdom) and hidden from assistive technology; flag emoji are not used, because Windows shows them as two letters. Each language name carries its own `lang`, and the current language is marked for assistive technology. At phone width the closed switch shows the flag alone, and its name stays available to screen readers. Every option is a touch target of at least 44 by 44 px. The component gallery (`/admin/components`) shows the switch in both states.
    - **Choosing a language** posts to `POST /language`, which returns the browser to the page it was on, now in the chosen language. The return address is checked exactly as `POST /theme` checks it, and anything refused returns to `/`, or to `/login` signed out.
    - **The language cookie:** the choice lives in one browser cookie, `sc_lang`, holding `uk` or `en`, set exactly like the theme cookie `sc_theme`: `Path=/`, `SameSite=Lax`, `HttpOnly`, `Secure` in production, kept for one year. Choosing a language always stores it, `uk` as well as `en`; the cookie is never deleted to mean the default, since the default can differ between environments. It holds only the choice: it is not linked to an account or a session, is not personal data, needs no consent, and no log line names it. Signing in, switching role and signing out neither read nor change it. A missing cookie, or anything other than `uk` or `en`, means the default language: Ukrainian, whatever the browser's `Accept-Language` says. The default is a setting, the environment variable `DEFAULT_LANGUAGE`, holding `uk` or `en`: unset means `uk`, so production needs no configuration, and any other value stops the application at startup, as other invalid settings do. The test configuration sets `en`, so the existing tests still read English; the tests of the default set `uk`. The language code is `uk` (ISO 639-1), never `ua`, in the cookie, in `DEFAULT_LANGUAGE`, in `<html lang>` and in the catalogue folders: `UA` is Ukraine's country code (ISO 3166-1), used only to name the flag, and `ua` is not a language code, so screen readers and Babel would not recognise it.
    - **Terminology:** a glossary in [ui-guidelines.md](ui-guidelines.md) fixes the Ukrainian word for every product term, and every page uses it. It covers at least the application's name, «Студентські змагання», chosen as the name that suits the college students who take part; the roles, with **Адміністратор** for the administrator; «заклад освіти» for an educational institution; and the statuses, access levels and the names of tabs and buttons. Participants come from schools and from colleges, so the words for the teacher and the student roles fit both: a word that belongs to only one of them is not used for a role, neither «учень» or «вчитель» (school) nor «студент» or «викладач» (college). The glossary proposes the words, for example «Педагог» and «Здобувач освіти», the terms Ukrainian education law uses for schools and colleges alike, and the product owner approves them.
    - **Writing in Ukrainian:** the rules of plain language apply to Ukrainian as they do to English. Typography: quotation marks «…», with „…“ for a quotation inside a quotation; the apostrophe ʼ; and the reader addressed as «ви».
    - **Names keep their form:** a name people entered (of a subject, region, institution, class, team, competition or person) is never declined, since the application cannot change its case. A message puts it, in quotation marks, after a noun that takes the case the sentence needs, so the name itself stays as typed: «Видалити предмет «Фізика»?», «Назад до регіону «Київ»». A person's name follows a colon: «Вилучити зі списку: Олена Коваленко?».
    - **No guessed gender:** a sentence about another person never uses a word that agrees with their gender: no past-tense verb, participle or adjective that changes with it. "X запрошує вас", not "X запросив вас"; «Запрошення надіслано», not «Запрошений». The reader, addressed as «ви», takes the plural forms, which show no gender.
    - **Layout:** Ukrainian text is about a quarter longer than English. Every page is checked in both languages and both themes, on a phone and on a desktop: nothing scrolls sideways, and no button, tab, badge or table header is cut off.
    - **The pinned frame is measured again:** the top bar gains the switch and the tabs get longer labels, so the frame is measured again in Ukrainian at 360 × 640 and 375 × 667, with the longest role label, on an administrator page and on a teacher page. `--sc-frame-clearance` and `--sc-frame-clearance-tabs` are raised if the frame outgrew them, and the frame still stays under a quarter of a 375 × 667 screen. The new measurements are recorded in the UI guidelines, as milestone 11.1's were.
    _Ukrainian names:_ Every list ordered by a name follows the Ukrainian alphabet, with Ґ after Г, Є after Е, and І and Ї after И: "Гончаренко, Ґудзь, Євтушенко, Іваненко, Їжакевич, Ющенко, Яковенко". Today Ґ, Є, І and Ї sort after Я, because the sort compares Unicode code points. The order is the same in both languages and comes from one shared sort key, still computed in Python, so SQLite and PostgreSQL agree. Latin names keep their order and come before Cyrillic ones; any other letter, such as the Russian Ё, Ъ, Ы and Э, comes after the letters of the Ukrainian alphabet, in Unicode order.
    - **The sort key** is computed when a list is sorted and is not stored: every list ordered by a name is already sorted in Python, and none is paged by the database. It ignores case and apostrophes and ranks the letters with a table of the Ukrainian alphabet written in the code; no sorting library is added. A person's name is ordered by last name, then first name, each with this key.
    - **Apostrophes:** the five apostrophe-like characters people type, `'`, `’`, `ʼ`, `‘` and `` ` ``, count as one and the same character when names are checked for duplicates, and are ignored when sorting, so "Мар'яна", "Мар’яна" and "Мар`яна" are the same name. Names are stored exactly as typed.
    - **The uniqueness key** stays stored, as today, and differs from the sort key: it treats the apostrophes as one character instead of ignoring them. A migration recomputes the stored uniqueness keys of every name that must be unique: subjects, regions, institutions, classes, teams and competitions. If two existing names now count as the same, it stops and names both, so an administrator renames one before upgrading.
    _Times:_ every time shown to people has one format in each language: "14 March 2027, 10:00" and «14 березня 2027, 10:00» (the day without a leading zero, the month in full, a 24-hour clock), and a date alone is "15 October 2026" and «15 жовтня 2026».
    - **On a page** a time is shown in the browser's time zone when it is known, with no label, as today. When it is not known (the script did not run), the time is shown in Kyiv time (`Europe/Kyiv`) and labelled so: "14 March 2027, 10:00 (Kyiv time)", «14 березня 2027, 10:00 (за київським часом)». This replaces milestone 12.1's fallback to UTC. A form whose zone is not known reads the time entered as Kyiv time, and its hint says "Time zone: Europe/Kyiv".
    - **Every page uses it:** the times and dates that still show the milestone 8 format ("2026-10-22 14:05 UTC", "2026-10-15"), on the profile's consent records, the invitation page's expiry and the administrator's **Teachers** list, move to this format and these zones.
    - **In an email** the reader's time zone is not known, so every time is in Kyiv time, labelled the same way.
    _Emails:_ The sign-in code and the data-deletion link are sent in the language of the browser that asked for them. The invitation is sent in Ukrainian followed by English, with a subject in both, since the invited person's language is not known. Times in emails follow the rules of _Times_.
    _Consent texts:_ Production holds no real users yet: nobody's consent has to be kept, and no real person has accepted a consent text. Version 1 of the teacher text and of the student text is therefore rewritten in place, as one bilingual text: the Ukrainian wording first, under the heading «Українською», then the English wording, under the heading "English", and in both, a sentence saying that the Ukrainian wording prevails if the two differ.
    - **The wordings:** the English wording is today's version 1, word for word, with only that sentence added. The Ukrainian wording is its translation, drafted during clarification of this milestone, and it is the binding one. The product owner approves it before it is published; if the review changes its meaning, the English wording is changed to match before publishing, so the two never say different things.
    - **The review does not hold up the work:** it runs while the rest of the milestone is built. The migration starts with the draft wordings, and the pinned SHA-256 values are set in the last commit, once the wording is approved; the pull request is merged only after that.
    - **Nothing else about consent changes:** no new table or column, no new version, and no re-consent. A role still has one current text; the consent record still points to it, and so proves agreement to both wordings, and the profile still shows the accepted text.
    - **Showing the text:** the invitation page and the profile show both wordings, whatever the chosen language, Ukrainian first. The page around them follows the chosen language. The English part is marked `lang="en"` so screen readers pronounce it correctly; the page finds it by its "English" heading line.
    - **The pinned texts:** the rewrite follows the precedent of milestone 11.1 (student version 1 was rewritten in place because no real users existed). The test that pins every published text's SHA-256 allows these two rewrites as well, and keeps every earlier wording of both versions pinned as superseded. Rewrites are numbered: the teams migration's `STUDENT_CONSENT_TEXT_V1_REWRITE` counts as rewrite 1, a later one is `<ROLE>_CONSENT_TEXT_V<n>_REWRITE_<k>`, and the highest number is the published text. Every earlier wording of a version stays pinned as superseded, and the test allows an exact number of rewrites per version: two for student version 1, one for teacher version 1, none for any other. The migration's downgrade restores the earlier wordings: the teacher's original and the student's rewrite 1.
    - **The last rewrite:** a rewrite in place is allowed only while production holds no real consent record. Milestone 12.5 must therefore reach production before the first real invitation is accepted. From then on a consent text is never rewritten: any change is a new version, published by a new migration.
    _How text is translated:_ the language is worked out once per request, and templates and Python code see the same one. The English wording is the message identifier, so specifications keep quoting the interface in English. Messages raised by services and schemas are message identifiers with named parameters, translated when the page is rendered or the email is written, never English sentences fixed when the module is imported. Every message is one whole sentence: none is assembled from pieces (`"Enter a " + field`, `legend + " date"`, `"as a " + role.lower()`), since Ukrainian words change form with their grammatical case. Counts take their plural form from the catalogue, never `"team" if n == 1 else "teams"`, since Ukrainian has three forms (1 команда, 2–4 команди, 5–20 команд, 21 команда, 22 команди). Month names come from the catalogue too, never from the process locale; Ukrainian needs them in the genitive ("14 березня 2027, 10:00"). Text meant for developers only (`template_error`, log lines) stays in English. Messages are extracted from the templates and the Python code with `pybabel extract` and merged with `pybabel update`; the catalogues are compiled when the Docker image is built and before the tests run, and the README says how to add or change a message.
    _Not translated:_ anything people enter is shown as entered, in whatever language they wrote it: names of subjects, regions, institutions, classes, teams and competitions, people's names, questions, reference answers, answers and any other text a person types. The sample questions seeded by milestone 3 stay as they are; they are edited or deleted in the question bank like any other question. The framework's own error texts are not translated either: the detail text Starlette gives an HTTP error ("Not Found", "Method Not Allowed") stays as it comes, inside the application's error page, whose frame, heading and links are translated; FastAPI's JSON response to a malformed request and the plain-text server error stay as they are. Also not translated:
    - **The component gallery** (`/admin/components`, outside production, for administrators only): its content stays in English, its messages are not in the catalogues, and the catalogue test leaves it out. The frame around it follows the chosen language, like every page.
    - **The email sender's name**, configured in `EMAIL_FROM`, stays as configured, in English.
    - **The browser's own messages**, such as those of a required or email field the browser checks before the form is sent, follow the browser's language, not the switch.
    _Acceptance:_ the product owner approves the Ukrainian glossary and the Ukrainian consent wording for teachers and students. Then:
    - With no language chosen, they open the sign-in page and see it in Ukrainian. Signed in as an administrator, a teacher and a student, they go through every tab and see every heading, button, label, hint, form error, status, count and date in Ukrainian, with the glossary's terms.
    - They choose **English** on any page and stay on that page, now in English; the choice is kept after signing out, and choosing **Українська** brings Ukrainian back. On a phone the switch shows only the flag and still works.
    - Lists of Ukrainian names follow the Ukrainian alphabet, and a name that differs from an existing one only by its apostrophe is refused as a duplicate.
    - With the browser's time zone known, times are shown in it with no label; with it unknown, in Kyiv time labelled «за київським часом» (or "Kyiv time"). The profile, the invitation page and the **Teachers** list use the same format as the competitions.
    - With Ukrainian chosen they receive the sign-in code in Ukrainian; with English, in English. **Delete my data** sends its link in the chosen language. They invite a teacher and a student and receive invitations in Ukrainian followed by English. Every time in an email is in Kyiv time, labelled.
    - The invitation page shows the consent text in Ukrainian followed by English, whichever language is chosen; after accepting, the profile shows the same bilingual text.
    _Test:_ the Ukrainian and English catalogues translate every message in use outside the component gallery, none marked fuzzy, with the same placeholders. Ukrainian plural forms are right for 1, 2, 5, 11, 21, 22 and 25, and dates use genitive month names. Every page renders in Ukrainian with `lang="uk"` when no language was chosen. The switch is in the top bar of every page, signed in and signed out, works without JavaScript, sets the cookie with the attributes of `sc_theme`, storing `uk` as well as `en`, and keeps the reader on the same page; a refused return address goes to `/` or `/login`, and an unknown cookie value means the default. `DEFAULT_LANGUAGE` unset means Ukrainian, and an invalid value stops the application at startup. Names sort in the Ukrainian order shown above, with Latin names first and other letters after the Ukrainian ones, and names of subjects, regions, institutions, classes, teams and competitions that differ only by one of the five apostrophes are refused as duplicates. Times use the format above in both languages; with no known zone a page shows Kyiv time labelled, a form reads its time as Kyiv time, and the dates of the profile, the invitation page and the **Teachers** list use the same format. The sign-in and data-deletion emails follow the chosen language, the invitation email holds both languages in its subject and its body, and every time in an email is Kyiv time, labelled. The current consent text of each role is its bilingual version 1, matching its new pinned SHA-256, with the earlier wordings pinned as superseded, rewrites numbered, and no rewrite beyond the allowed counts accepted; its English wording is today's version 1 plus the "Ukrainian prevails" sentence. The invitation page and the profile show both wordings in either language, with the English part marked `lang="en"`, and an acceptance records version 1. 13. **Taking a competition (student).** List of the competitions the student's teams take part in; a competition's questions open and its answers are accepted only while it is **In progress**, from its start until a teacher stops it, since a competition has no duration; the students of a team enter the team's text answers, one per question per team. These are the same answers a teacher enters in milestone 12.3: a student has no answers of their own, every member of the team sees the team's answers, and the last save wins. The student's lists of upcoming and past competitions show each competition's subject. A competition of a deactivated subject that is not finished is hidden from students and cannot be opened.
    _Test:_ a team's answers are saved and shown to every member of the team and to a teacher with Write access to its institution; the competition is not accessible before its start, after it is stopped or cancelled, or while its subject is deactivated.

14. **Answer evaluation via LLM.** Each team's answer receives a proposed grade, a whole number from 0 to 10 on the scale of the manual grade (milestone 12.4), that takes level of detail into account; the evaluation is saved to the database. The proposed grade is the foundation of the answer's grade, and a teacher with **Can manage competitions** can still adjust it by hand; the stored grade, proposed or adjusted, is the one that counts, and a team's score stays the sum of its grades. Answers and reference answers are mostly in Ukrainian (milestone 12.5), and the evaluation is designed and tested on Ukrainian answers; any text it shows to teachers is in the language of the answers. The details are refined after milestone 12.4 is shown to the product owner.
    _Test:_ evaluation returns a structured result and it is persisted; a proposed grade can be adjusted by hand, and the team's score follows the adjusted grade.

15. **Best answer selection + results.** For each question, the best answer is selected from the teams' answers and saved. Each team's score, the sum of its stored grades (milestone 12.4), and its place, worked out from the scores, are calculated and displayed. The winner is the team with the highest score. The details are refined after milestone 12.4 is shown to the product owner.
    _Kept for the record:_ a completed competition keeps showing every one of its questions and its subject, even after the question was deleted from the question bank or the subject was deactivated (milestone 11), and every one of its teams with its answers, even after the team was deleted (milestone 11.1).
    _Who sees results:_ a teacher sees the results, grades and scores of the teams of their own institutions, with Read or Write access (milestone 8); both see their answers read-only (milestone 12.4). Results of teams from other institutions are not shown to them, except that a teacher with **Can manage competitions** sees the grades and scores of every team of a competition on its **Grades** page, to grade them (milestone 12.4). A student sees the results of their own teams.
    _Test:_ the best answer is determined, the results page works. The results still show a question deleted after the competition, a subject deactivated after it and a team deleted after it. A teacher with Read access sees the results of that institution's teams and none from institutions they are not linked to.

16. **Polish and hardening.** Results dashboards, E2E tests with Playwright, error handling, security. (Optional stretch goal: move part of the UI to React.)
