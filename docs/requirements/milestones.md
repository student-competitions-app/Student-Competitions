# Milestones

The milestone ladder for the product described in [product-requirements.md](product-requirements.md), built with the stack in [technical-requirements.md](technical-requirements.md). Each milestone is delivered as one Spec Kit feature.

The ladder follows a "walking skeleton" approach: first ship an empty skeleton to the internet, then grow it with vertical slices. Each step ends with a working, testable and meaningful result.

**Delivered milestones** are short summaries of what the product does after them. Their specification in [`specs/`](../../specs/) is the source of truth for details, and the summary is updated only if the product behaviour changes. **Upcoming milestones** hold the full scope, refined until every detail is clear, and are the input for `speckit-specify`.

## Delivered

1. **Hello World locally.** _Spec:_ [001-hello-world-page](../../specs/001-hello-world-page/spec.md)
   - A server-rendered home page that runs on a developer's machine, with a README for setup, running and testing.

2. **Deploy Hello World to the internet + CI/CD.** _Spec:_ [002-public-deploy-cicd](../../specs/002-public-deploy-cicd/spec.md)
   - The application is public over HTTPS. Pull requests are gated by tests and style checks, every merge to `main` is deployed automatically, and a status address reports health and version.

3. **Database + first entity.** _Spec:_ [003-database-questions](../../specs/003-database-questions/spec.md)
   - A persistent database with the first entity, the question (text and reference answer). The home page shows a database status line and stays up when the database is unreachable.

4. **Email code authentication.** _Spec:_ [004-email-otp-auth](../../specs/004-email-otp-auth/spec.md)
   - Passwordless sign-in with a one-time emailed code, only for users already known to the application, with rate limits, no account enumeration and 14-day sessions. Every page except sign-in, status and static files requires sign-in.

5. **Roles and authorization.** _Spec:_ [005-roles-authorization](../../specs/005-roles-authorization/spec.md)
   - Administrator, teacher and student roles. A person with several roles chooses one and can switch from the header; access follows the current role only and is denied by default.

6. **Administrator area and subjects.** _Spec:_ [006-admin-area](../../specs/006-admin-area/spec.md)
   - A tabbed administrator area. Administrators create, rename, deactivate, activate and delete (while unused) subjects.

7. **Administrator area: educational institutions.** _Spec:_ [007-region-and-institutions-management](../../specs/007-region-and-institutions-management/spec.md)
   - Administrators manage regions and the educational institutions in them with the same actions as subjects. Only an active institution in an active region can be selected.

8. **User management with consent: teachers.** _Spec:_ [008-teacher-management-consent](../../specs/008-teacher-management-consent/spec.md)
   - Administrators invite teachers by email; nothing is stored until the teacher accepts the versioned consent text. Teachers are linked to institutions with **Read** or **Write** access and have a competition-management setting. Every user has a profile with **Withdraw consent**, and **Delete my data** works without signing in.

9. **User management with consent: classes and students.** _Spec:_ [009-classes-students-management](../../specs/009-classes-students-management/spec.md)
   - A teacher area with a **Students** tab. Teachers with Write access manage classes and invite, edit, move and remove students through the same consent flow; Read access is view-only. Deactivating a region, institution or class blocks the students below it.

10. **Professional design system and UI guidelines.** _Spec:_ [010-design-system-ui-guidelines](../../specs/010-design-system-ui-guidelines/spec.md)
    - A visual identity, one shared page frame, a component library used by every page, and **Light** / **Dark** / **Device** themes. The binding UI guidelines are in [ui-guidelines.md](ui-guidelines.md), with a component gallery at `/admin/components` and an automated check.

10.1. **Feedback while a page loads, and a frame that stays in view.** _Spec:_ [010.1-loading-feedback-pinned-frame](../../specs/010.1-loading-feedback-pinned-frame/spec.md)
   - A loading bar for slow navigations, a busy state on the button that sent a form, and a top bar and area tabs pinned on tall screens. Everything works without JavaScript.

11. **Question bank (teacher).** _Spec:_ [011-question-bank](../../specs/011-question-bank/spec.md)
    - A **Questions** tab where every teacher browses the shared question bank by subject, paginated. Teachers with **Can manage competitions** add, edit and soft-delete questions.

11.1. **Teams (teacher).** _Spec:_ [011.1-teacher-teams](../../specs/011.1-teacher-teams/spec.md)
   - A **Teams** tab. Teachers with Write access create, rename and soft-delete teams of an institution and add and remove its students as members. Memberships are personal data, shown on the profile and erased with the student's data.

12.1. **Creating, editing and cancelling competitions (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.1-teacher-competitions](../../specs/012.1-teacher-competitions/spec.md)
   - A **Competitions** tab listing competitions by subject and status, paginated. Teachers with **Can manage competitions** add competitions, edit upcoming ones and cancel upcoming or in-progress ones. The status (**Upcoming**, **In progress**, **Finished**, **Cancelled**) is derived; times are stored in UTC and shown in the browser's time zone.

12.2. **Questions and teams of a competition (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.2-competition-questions-teams](../../specs/012.2-competition-questions-teams/spec.md)
   - Every competition row has an actions menu with **Questions** and **Teams**, which every teacher reads. Teachers with **Can manage competitions** add questions (copied from the bank when added) and teams of any institution, one region at a time, to an **Upcoming** competition, and remove them. Teams are listed in region boxes. Deleting a team takes it out of every competition that has not finished.

12.3. **Entering a team's answers (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.3-team-answers](../../specs/012.3-team-answers/spec.md)
   - On an **In progress** competition every teacher has **Answers**: their own institutions' teams in region boxes with each team's progress ("3 of 6"). With **Write** access a teacher enters a team's answers on one answer sheet, one text answer per question, saved at once; the last save wins. **Read** access shows only the progress (from milestone 12.4 it also opens the answers read-only). Deleting a team deletes its answers in every competition that has not finished.

12.4. **Finishing a competition and grading its answers by hand (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.4-finish-and-grade](../../specs/012.4-finish-and-grade/spec.md)
   - Teachers with **Can manage competitions** finish an **In progress** competition (**Finish competition**, a primary confirmation with **Keep in progress**); it is **Finished** for good and its answers can no longer be entered. On a **Finished** competition they open **Grades**: every team, whatever their links, with **Answered**, **Graded** and **Score**, an **Ungraded only** filter, and a grading sheet per team with the reference answers and one whole-number grade from 0 to 10 per answer, saved at once; the last save wins, and a team's score is the sum of its grades. On the **Competitions** tab **Read** access now opens a team's answers read-only; after finishing, the **Answers** pages are read-only for the teachers of a team's institution and show its grades and score.

## Upcoming

12. **Competitions (12.x).** A group of milestones, not a milestone of its own: it has no spec and no test. Together the 12.x milestones build the teacher's side of competitions: creating, editing and cancelling them, choosing their questions and teams, entering the teams' answers, grading the answers by hand, and stopping them. Milestones 12.5 and 12.6 are not about competitions: they make the whole application bilingual, Ukrainian and English, before the student's side is built, so every later milestone is written in both languages from the start. Taking a competition (milestone 13), answer evaluation by an LLM (milestone 14) and results (milestone 15) come after.

12.5. **Translatable interface and the language switch.** The application is used in Ukrainian educational institutions, so it must speak Ukrainian. This milestone prepares everything for it without changing what people read: every text the application shows or sends becomes translatable, Ukrainian names sort and compare correctly, and the language switch is built. The interface still reads in English, exactly as before, until milestone 12.6 adds the Ukrainian translation and puts the switch in the top bar. The stack does not change: gettext catalogues managed with Babel on top of the existing Jinja2 templates (`jinja2.ext.i18n`); Babel is the only new dependency.
    _Translatable text:_ every page, component, form error, notice, error page, page title, visually hidden text and `aria-label`, the status, role and access-level labels, the dates and the three emails take their wording from the catalogue of the request's language. The language is worked out once per request, and templates and Python code see the same one. The English wording is the message identifier, so specifications keep quoting the interface in English. Messages raised by services and schemas are message identifiers with named parameters, translated when the page is rendered, never English sentences fixed when the module is imported. Every message is one whole sentence: none is assembled from pieces (`"Enter a " + field`, `legend + " date"`, `"as a " + role.lower()`), since Ukrainian words change form with their grammatical case. Counts take their plural form from the catalogue, never `"team" if n == 1 else "teams"`, since Ukrainian has three forms (1 команда, 2–4 команди, 5–20 команд, 21 команда, 22 команди). Month names come from the catalogue too, never from the process locale; Ukrainian needs them in the genitive ("14 березня 2027, 10:00"). Text meant for developers only (`template_error`, log lines) stays in English.
    _Not translated:_ what people enter is shown as entered, in whatever language they wrote it: names of subjects, regions, institutions, classes and teams, people's names, questions, reference answers and answers.
    _Ukrainian names:_ every list sorted by name (regions, institutions, subjects, teachers, students, teams and a teacher's institution links) follows the Ukrainian alphabet, with Ґ after Г, Є after Е, and І and Ї after И: "Гончаренко, Ґудзь, Євтушенко, Іваненко, Їжакевич, Ющенко, Яковенко". Today Ґ, Є, І and Ї sort after Я, because the sort compares Unicode code points. The order comes from one shared sort key, still computed in Python, so SQLite and PostgreSQL agree; Latin names keep their order and come before Cyrillic ones. The three apostrophes people type in Ukrainian names, `'`, `’` and `ʼ`, count as the same character when names are checked for duplicates, and are ignored when sorting, so "Мар'яна" and "Мар’яна" are the same name. Names are stored exactly as typed. A migration recomputes the stored uniqueness keys; if two existing names now count as the same, it stops and names both, so an administrator renames one before upgrading.
    _Language switch:_ built in this milestone and shown only in the component gallery (`/admin/components`), in both states; milestone 12.6 places it in the top bar. It looks like a combobox: it shows the current language's flag and name, and when opened lists both languages, each with its flag and its name written in that language: the Ukrainian flag with **Українська**, and the United Kingdom flag with **English**. It is a native `<details>` menu with a small form, like the user menu, so it works without JavaScript. The flags are small SVG images in `app/static/img/`, hidden from assistive technology. Flag emoji are not used, because Windows shows them as two letters. Each language name carries its own `lang`, and the current language is marked for assistive technology. At phone width the closed switch shows the flag alone, and its name stays available to screen readers. Every option is a touch target of at least 44 by 44 px. A choice posts to `POST /language`, which returns the browser to the page it was on; the return address is checked exactly as `POST /theme` checks it, and anything refused returns to `/`, or to `/login` signed out.
    _The language cookie:_ the choice lives in one browser cookie, `sc_lang`, holding `uk` or `en`, kept for one year and `HttpOnly`. It holds only the choice: it is not linked to an account or a session, is not personal data, needs no consent, and no log line names it. Signing in, switching role and signing out neither read nor change it. A missing cookie, or anything other than `uk` or `en`, means the default language, which is a setting: English in this milestone. The language code is `uk` (ISO 639-1), never `ua`, which is the country code, in the cookie, in `<html lang>` and in the catalogue folders.
    _Catalogue workflow:_ messages are extracted from the templates and the Python code with `pybabel extract` and merged with `pybabel update`. The catalogues are compiled when the Docker image is built and before the tests run. The README says how to add or change a message.
    _Test:_ every message in use is in the English catalogue, with the same placeholders; plural forms come from the catalogue; the switch posts, sets the cookie and returns to the same page, and a refused return address goes to `/` or `/login`; an unknown cookie value means the default language. Names sort in the Ukrainian order shown above, and names that differ only by apostrophe are refused as duplicates. The existing tests pass unchanged, since they run in English.

12.6. **Ukrainian interface.** Everything the application shows or sends is translated into Ukrainian. Ukrainian becomes the default language, and the language switch of milestone 12.5 moves into the top bar, so anyone can choose English.
    _Terminology:_ a glossary in [ui-guidelines.md](ui-guidelines.md) fixes the Ukrainian word for every product term, and every page uses it. It covers at least the application's name (proposed: «Учнівські змагання»); the roles **Адміністратор**, **Вчитель** and **Учень** (the classes are school years 1–12, so a student is «учень», not «студент»); «заклад освіти» for an educational institution; and the statuses, access levels and the names of tabs and buttons. The rules of plain language apply to Ukrainian as they do to English. Ukrainian typography: quotation marks «…», the apostrophe ʼ, and the reader addressed as «ви». A sentence about another person never uses a past-tense verb, which would guess their gender: "X запрошує вас", not "X запросив вас".
    _Default language:_ Ukrainian, whatever the browser's `Accept-Language` says; English only when chosen. The test suite keeps English as its default through the setting, so existing tests still read English.
    _Language switch in the top bar:_ on every page, signed in or signed out, including sign-in, invitation, data deletion and error pages, placed before the user menu. Each page is shown in the chosen language, and `<html lang>` names it.
    _Consent texts:_ every consent text exists in Ukrainian and in English. A consent text gets a language, and a role's text is unique by version and language. The invitation page shows the text in the reader's language, and the consent record keeps the exact text accepted, which the profile shows. The Ukrainian text is the binding one and is reviewed by the product owner before it is published; each published text keeps its pinned SHA-256. The existing records, made with English version 1, stay valid.
    _Emails:_ the sign-in code and the data-deletion link are sent in the language of the browser that asked for them. The invitation is sent in Ukrainian followed by English, with a subject in both, since the invited person's language is not known.
    _Sample questions:_ the questions seeded by milestone 3 stay as they are; they are edited or deleted in the question bank like any other question.
    _Layout:_ Ukrainian text is about a quarter longer than English. Every page is checked in both languages and both themes, on a phone and on a desktop: nothing scrolls sideways, and no button, tab, badge or table header is cut off.
    _Test:_ the Ukrainian catalogue translates every message, none marked fuzzy, with the same placeholders as the English one. Ukrainian plural forms are right for 1, 2, 5, 11, 21, 22 and 25, and dates use genitive month names. Every page renders in Ukrainian with `lang="uk"`. The switch is in the top bar of every page, signed in and signed out, works without JavaScript and keeps the reader on the same page. Ukrainian is shown when no language was chosen. The invitation page shows the consent text in the reader's language, and the consent record points to the text shown. The sign-in email follows the chosen language, and the invitation email holds both languages.

13. **Taking a competition (student).** List of the competitions the student's teams take part in; a competition's questions open and its answers are accepted only while it is **In progress**, from its start until a teacher stops it, since a competition has no duration; the students of a team enter the team's text answers, one per question per team. These are the same answers a teacher enters in milestone 12.3: a student has no answers of their own, every member of the team sees the team's answers, and the last save wins. The student's lists of upcoming and past competitions show each competition's subject. A competition of a deactivated subject that is not finished is hidden from students and cannot be opened.
    _Test:_ a team's answers are saved and shown to every member of the team and to a teacher with Write access to its institution; the competition is not accessible before its start, after it is stopped or cancelled, or while its subject is deactivated.

14. **Answer evaluation via LLM.** Each team's answer receives a proposed grade, a whole number from 0 to 10 on the scale of the manual grade (milestone 12.4), that takes level of detail into account; the evaluation is saved to the database. The proposed grade is the foundation of the answer's grade, and a teacher with **Can manage competitions** can still adjust it by hand; the stored grade, proposed or adjusted, is the one that counts, and a team's score stays the sum of its grades. Answers and reference answers are mostly in Ukrainian (milestone 12.6), and the evaluation is designed and tested on Ukrainian answers; any text it shows to teachers is in the language of the answers. The details are refined after milestone 12.4 is shown to the product owner.
    _Test:_ evaluation returns a structured result and it is persisted; a proposed grade can be adjusted by hand, and the team's score follows the adjusted grade.

15. **Best answer selection + results.** For each question, the best answer is selected from the teams' answers and saved. Each team's score, the sum of its stored grades (milestone 12.4), and its place, worked out from the scores, are calculated and displayed. The winner is the team with the highest score. The details are refined after milestone 12.4 is shown to the product owner.
    _Kept for the record:_ a completed competition keeps showing every one of its questions and its subject, even after the question was deleted from the question bank or the subject was deactivated (milestone 11), and every one of its teams with its answers, even after the team was deleted (milestone 11.1).
    _Who sees results:_ a teacher sees the results, grades and scores of the teams of their own institutions, with Read or Write access (milestone 8); both see their answers read-only (milestone 12.4). Results of teams from other institutions are not shown to them, except that a teacher with **Can manage competitions** sees the grades and scores of every team of a competition on its **Grades** page, to grade them (milestone 12.4). A student sees the results of their own teams.
    _Test:_ the best answer is determined, the results page works. The results still show a question deleted after the competition, a subject deactivated after it and a team deleted after it. A teacher with Read access sees the results of that institution's teams and none from institutions they are not linked to.

16. **Polish and hardening.** Results dashboards, E2E tests with Playwright, error handling, security. (Optional stretch goal: move part of the UI to React.)
