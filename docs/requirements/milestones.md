# Milestones

The milestone ladder for the product described in [product-requirements.md](product-requirements.md), built with the stack in [technical-requirements.md](technical-requirements.md). Each milestone is delivered as one Spec Kit feature.

The ladder follows a "walking skeleton" approach: first ship an empty skeleton to the internet, then grow it with vertical slices. Each step ends with a working, testable and meaningful result.

**Delivered milestones** are short summaries of what the product does after them. Their specification in [`specs/`](../../specs/) is the source of truth for details, and the summary is updated only if the product behaviour changes. **Upcoming milestones** hold the full scope, refined until every detail is clear, and are the input for `speckit-specify`.

## Delivered

1. **Hello World locally.** _Spec:_ [001-hello-world-page](../../specs/001-hello-world-page/spec.md)
   - A server-rendered home page that runs on a developer's machine, with a README for setup, running and testing.

2. **Deploy Hello World to the internet + CI/CD.** _Spec:_ [002-public-deploy-cicd](../../specs/002-public-deploy-cicd/spec.md)
   - The application is public over HTTPS. Pull requests are gated by tests and style checks, every merge to `main` is deployed automatically, and a status address reports health and version.

3. **Database + first entity.** _Spec:_ [003-database-questions](../../specs/003-database-questions/spec.md)
   - A persistent database with the first entity, the question (text and reference answer). A database status line (in the footer of the administrator area and the student's home page since milestone 12.6), and its page stays up when the database is unreachable.

4. **Email code authentication.** _Spec:_ [004-email-otp-auth](../../specs/004-email-otp-auth/spec.md)
   - Passwordless sign-in with a one-time emailed code, only for users already known to the application, with rate limits, no account enumeration and 14-day sessions. Every page except sign-in, status and static files requires sign-in.

5. **Roles and authorization.** _Spec:_ [005-roles-authorization](../../specs/005-roles-authorization/spec.md)
   - Administrator, teacher and student roles. A person with several roles chooses one and can switch from the header; access follows the current role only and is denied by default.

6. **Administrator area and subjects.** _Spec:_ [006-admin-area](../../specs/006-admin-area/spec.md)
   - A tabbed administrator area. Administrators create, rename, deactivate, activate and delete (while unused) subjects.

7. **Administrator area: educational institutions.** _Spec:_ [007-region-and-institutions-management](../../specs/007-region-and-institutions-management/spec.md)
   - Administrators manage regions and the educational institutions in them with the same actions as subjects. Only an active institution in an active region can be selected.

8. **User management with consent: teachers.** _Spec:_ [008-teacher-management-consent](../../specs/008-teacher-management-consent/spec.md)
   - Administrators invite teachers by email; nothing is stored until the teacher accepts the versioned consent text. Teachers are linked to institutions with **Read** or **Write** access and have a competition-management setting. Every user has a profile with **Withdraw consent**, and **Delete my data** works without signing in.

9. **User management with consent: classes and students.** _Spec:_ [009-classes-students-management](../../specs/009-classes-students-management/spec.md)
   - A teacher area with a **Students** tab. Teachers with Write access manage classes and invite, edit, move and remove students through the same consent flow; Read access is view-only. Deactivating a region, institution or class blocks the students below it.

10. **Professional design system and UI guidelines.** _Spec:_ [010-design-system-ui-guidelines](../../specs/010-design-system-ui-guidelines/spec.md)
    - A visual identity, one shared page frame, a component library used by every page, and **Light** / **Dark** / **Device** themes. The binding UI guidelines are in [ui-guidelines.md](ui-guidelines.md), with a component gallery at `/admin/components` and an automated check.

10.1. **Feedback while a page loads, and a frame that stays in view.** _Spec:_ [010.1-loading-feedback-pinned-frame](../../specs/010.1-loading-feedback-pinned-frame/spec.md)
   - A loading bar for slow navigations, a busy state on the button that sent a form, and a top bar and area tabs pinned on tall screens. Everything works without JavaScript.

11. **Question bank (teacher).** _Spec:_ [011-question-bank](../../specs/011-question-bank/spec.md)
    - A **Questions** tab where every teacher browses the question bank by subject, paginated. Teachers with **Can manage competitions** add, edit and soft-delete questions, and from milestone 12.9 only they see its additional questions.

11.1. **Teams (teacher).** _Spec:_ [011.1-teacher-teams](../../specs/011.1-teacher-teams/spec.md)
   - A **Teams** tab. Teachers with Write access create, rename and soft-delete teams of an institution and add and remove its students as members. Memberships are personal data, shown on the profile and erased with the student's data.

12.1. **Creating, editing and cancelling competitions (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.1-teacher-competitions](../../specs/012.1-teacher-competitions/spec.md)
   - A **Competitions** tab listing competitions by subject and status, paginated. Teachers with **Can manage competitions** add competitions, edit upcoming ones and cancel upcoming or in-progress ones. The status (**Upcoming**, **In progress**, **Finished**, **Cancelled**) is derived; times are stored in UTC and shown in the browser's time zone.

12.2. **Questions and teams of a competition (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.2-competition-questions-teams](../../specs/012.2-competition-questions-teams/spec.md)
   - Every competition row has an actions menu with **Questions** and **Teams**, which every teacher reads. Teachers with **Can manage competitions** add questions (copied from the bank when added) and teams of any institution, one region at a time, to an **Upcoming** competition, and remove them. Teams are listed in region boxes. Deleting a team takes it out of every competition that has not finished.

12.3. **Entering a team's answers (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.3-team-answers](../../specs/012.3-team-answers/spec.md)
   - On an **In progress** competition every teacher has **Answers**: their own institutions' teams in region boxes with each team's progress ("3 of 6"). With **Write** access a teacher enters a team's answers on one answer sheet, one text answer per question, saved at once; the last save wins. **Read** access shows only the progress (from milestone 12.4 it also opens the answers read-only). Deleting a team deletes its answers in every competition that has not finished.

12.4. **Finishing a competition and grading its answers by hand (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.4-finish-and-grade](../../specs/012.4-finish-and-grade/spec.md)
   - Teachers with **Can manage competitions** finish an **In progress** competition (**Finish competition**, a primary confirmation with **Keep in progress**); it is **Finished** for good and its answers can no longer be entered. On a **Finished** competition they open **Grades**: every team, whatever their links, with **Answered**, **Graded** and **Score**, an **Ungraded only** filter, and a grading sheet per team with the reference answers and one whole-number grade from 0 to 10 per answer, saved at once; the last save wins, and a team's score is the sum of its grades. On the **Competitions** tab **Read** access now opens a team's answers read-only; after finishing, the **Answers** pages are read-only for the teachers of a team's institution and show its grades and score.

12.5. **Ukrainian localisation.** _Spec:_ [012.5-ukrainian-localisation](../../specs/012.5-ukrainian-localisation/spec.md)
   - Every page opens in Ukrainian; a language switch in the top bar changes it to English and back, works without JavaScript and is kept in a cookie. Messages live in gettext catalogues managed with Babel, compiled for the image and the tests. Times without a known browser zone are shown in Kyiv time, labelled. Sign-in and data-deletion emails follow the chosen language; invitations hold Ukrainian then English. Both consent texts are bilingual version 1, Ukrainian first and prevailing. Names sort in the Ukrainian alphabet, and names that differ only by their apostrophe are duplicates. What people enter is never translated.

12.6. **Each role lands in its own area.** _Spec:_ [012.6-role-landing-area](../../specs/012.6-role-landing-area/spec.md)
   - `/` takes an administrator or a teacher straight to the area of their current role, from sign-in, the role choice, the role switch and the brand link; a student still gets the home page. The placeholder **Staff area** is gone and `/staff` answers 404. The database line is in the footer of the administrator area pages and the student's home page, and the back link of the profile and error pages leads to the current role's area.

12.7. **The new logo.** _Spec:_ [012.7-new-logo](../../specs/012.7-new-logo/spec.md)
   - The amber spark is replaced by the engraved emblem: the letters ЯНМ, solid, in a diamond of engraved flourishes. The top bar shows it in light ink, 44 px at every width, as tall as the language switch; elsewhere the logo mark follows the theme, also without JavaScript. The browser and device icons show the letters ЯНМ alone on deep blue, and an icon added to a home screen opens the application in the browser. `scripts/make_logo.py` (the non-default `logo` group: Pillow and numpy) makes every committed image from `docs/brand/logo-source.png`. The product owner approved the look after four review rounds.

12.8. **Publishing a competition's results as PDF files (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.8-publishing-results](../../specs/012.8-publishing-results/spec.md)
   - Teachers with **Can manage competitions** publish the results of a **Finished** competition once every team is fully graded (**Publish results**, a primary confirmation with **Keep unpublished**); it becomes **Published** for good, a new status with its own badge and filter, and its grading sheets turn read-only. Publishing makes two PDF files, Ukrainian and English, and stores them in the database as the snapshot that confirms the results: the logo, the dates in Kyiv time, the maximum score, and a table per region of every team's institution, name and score, deleted teams included, on numbered pages. Every teacher downloads the file of the current language from the actions menu (**Download results**), named "<name> <YYYY-MM-DD>.pdf". The files are made with fpdf2 and embedded Open Sans; the product owner approved their design after two review rounds.

12.9. **Additional questions in the question bank (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.9-additional-questions](../../specs/012.9-additional-questions/spec.md)
   - Teachers with **Can manage competitions** mark a question of the bank as an **additional question** with the checkbox **Additional question** on its add and edit form; questions already in the bank start unmarked, and a deleted question or one of a deactivated subject keeps its mark. Only these teachers see the additional questions: on the **Questions** tab, on **Add questions** and on a competition's **Questions** page, each with the badge **Additional** (`info`, new to the component library with the text card's optional badge) beside its number, read from the bank so it follows a later change of the mark. Every other teacher gets the question bank without them, left out when the database is read, so the list, the count and the pages show no trace of them, and every single-question address answers the same "access denied" whatever the number. A competition's copy of a question takes no copy of the mark. From milestone 12.10 only these teachers read a competition's **Questions** page, and the study materials leave the additional questions out.

12.10. **A competition's questions only after it starts, and study materials (teacher).** _Spec:_ [012.10-questions-study-materials](../../specs/012.10-questions-study-materials/spec.md)
   - A competition's **Questions** page, with its reference answers, is offered only to teachers with **Can manage competitions**, in every status; every other teacher gets "access denied" for it and every page under it, the same for a missing competition, and **Teams** stays offered to everyone. On an **In progress**, **Finished** or **Published** competition every teacher has **Download questions**: a PDF file of the question texts in their order, numbered from 1, additional ones included, without reference answers, bank numbers or the **Additional** mark, made at every download and named "<name> <YYYY-MM-DD> questions.pdf". Once an active subject is chosen on the **Competitions** tab, every teacher has **Download study materials**: a PDF file of every question of the bank with its reference answer, oldest first, numbered from 1, without additional or deleted questions, named "<subject> <YYYY-MM-DD> study materials.pdf"; the last file per subject and language is kept in memory and given again only while nothing it shows has changed. Both files follow the design of the results file in the current site language, and the product owner approved them and the link in one review round.

## Upcoming

12. **Competitions (12.x).** A group of milestones, not a milestone of its own: it has no spec and no test. Together the 12.x milestones build the teacher's side of competitions: creating, editing and cancelling them, choosing their questions and teams, entering the teams' answers, grading the answers by hand, stopping them, and publishing their results as PDF files. Milestone 12.5 is not about competitions: it makes the whole application bilingual, Ukrainian and English, so every later milestone is written in both languages from the start. Milestone 12.6 is not about competitions either: it takes administrators and teachers straight to their own area. Neither is milestone 12.7, which replaces the logo. Milestone 12.9 marks the additional questions of the question bank, which nobody but the teachers with **Can manage competitions** sees before a competition that holds them starts, so the bank can be given to participants without them. Milestone 12.10 shows a competition's questions with their reference answers only to teachers with **Can manage competitions**, in every status, and once it starts lets every teacher download their texts, without the reference answers, for the teams as a PDF file, and lets every teacher download a subject's question bank with its reference answers, without the additional questions, as study materials for the students. Milestone 12.11 lets administrators export a subject's question bank to a CSV file and import a CSV file that replaces it, so a bank of a hundred questions or more is prepared in a spreadsheet instead of one question at a time. Answer evaluation by an LLM (milestone 13) and results (milestone 14) come after.

12.11. **Exporting and importing a subject's questions as CSV files (administrator).** Today a question enters the bank only through the teacher's add form (milestone 11), one at a time, and a bank holds a hundred questions or more per subject (milestone 12.10). From this milestone an administrator exports every question of a subject to a CSV file, edits it in a spreadsheet, and imports it again, or has an AI assistant such as Claude Desktop write a new file from the format described in the README and imports that; the import replaces the subject's questions with those of the file. Only administrators have both, on the **Subjects** tab of the administrator area (milestone 6); teachers keep the **Questions** tab as it is, and students get nothing.
    _Where:_ every row of the **Subjects** tab gets two more actions, **Export questions** and **Import questions**, for an active and a deactivated subject alike, so an administrator can prepare the bank of a subject before activating it. The table also gets a **Questions** column with the number of the subject's questions, the additional ones included and the deleted ones left out, so the administrator sees the result of an import in the list. Five actions no longer fit a phone card, so a row's actions move into the actions menu of milestone 12.1 (UI guidelines, "Actions menu"), in this order: **Rename**, **Export questions**, **Import questions**, **Deactivate** or **Activate**, **Delete**; **Import questions**, **Deactivate** and **Delete** are destructive entries. A menu entry is always a link to a page, never a `POST`, so **Deactivate** and **Activate**, which today act straight from the row, each open a confirmation page first, as **Deactivate** does for a region and an institution (milestone 7): "Deactivate “<subject>”?", destructive, saying that the subject's questions and every one of its competitions that is not finished are hidden from teachers until it is activated again; and "Activate “<subject>”?", primary, saying that they are shown again; each with **Cancel**, and each returns to the tab. Nothing else about deactivating or activating a subject changes (milestone 6). A subject that does not exist answers 404 with the page "Subject not found" (milestone 6), for both actions. Teachers and students opening either address get the "access denied" page (milestone 5).
    _Export questions:_ a link marked as a download, like **Download results** (milestone 12.8): the exported file downloads straight away, with no page in between, the administrator stays on the tab and the loading bar of milestone 10.1 does not start; it works without JavaScript. The export holds every question of the subject's bank, oldest first, the additional questions included and the deleted questions left out; a subject with no questions gives a file with the header row only. It reads the bank as it is at the export and is not stored. The file is named after the subject and the date of the export in Kyiv time, with the words "question bank" in the current site language, "<subject> <YYYY-MM-DD> question bank.csv" (e.g. "History 2026-11-25 question bank.csv"); characters a file name cannot hold are replaced, as in milestone 12.8.
    _The CSV format:_ one header row and one row per question, with three columns in this order: `question`, `answer` and `additional`. The header row is the same in both site languages, so a file exported in one language imports in the other. `question` and `answer` hold the question text and the reference answer exactly as stored, line breaks included; `additional` holds `1` for an additional question (milestone 12.9) and `0` for every other. The export is UTF-8 with a byte-order mark, separated by commas, with fields quoted where needed (RFC 4180), so a spreadsheet such as Excel or LibreOffice opens it with Cyrillic text intact and the line breaks inside a cell. The import accepts what such a spreadsheet saves back: UTF-8 with or without the byte-order mark, separated by commas or by semicolons (as Excel saves CSV with a Ukrainian regional setting), with `\r\n` or `\n` line endings. The separator is taken from the header row: a comma in it means commas; otherwise a semicolon means semicolons; a header with neither is wrong. The header names are matched ignoring case and the spaces around them; columns must be in the order above. A row is a record of the file, as a spreadsheet shows it: a field with line breaks inside stays one row. A row is empty, and skipped, when every one of its fields is empty or only spaces: a blank line, or `;;` as Excel writes it for an empty formatted row; a row with only some fields empty is checked like any other. Spaces around the value of `additional` are ignored, as around the header names, and an empty cell means `0`; `question` and `answer` are cleaned by the rules of the add form (below). A quote inside a field that is not quoted is kept as a character. A text that starts with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` is exported as it is, without a prefix, so the file imports back unchanged; a spreadsheet may read such a cell as a formula, an accepted risk, since only teachers write the texts and only administrators get the file. The question number of the bank is never in the file, so the file says nothing about the questions it will replace.
    _The format in the README:_ a question bank is often written outside the application, by a standalone request to an AI assistant such as Claude Desktop that has no access to the application, its code or its database. So the milestone adds a section "Question bank CSV files" to the README, written for such an assistant: given only that section and a request such as "write 150 history questions with their answers", it writes a file that imports without an error. The section stands on its own and holds every rule of the import, stated exactly: the header row and the column order; UTF-8, with the byte-order mark recommended; commas as the separator; how to quote a field (quoting every field is safe), double a quote inside it and keep a line break inside it (RFC 4180); the `.csv` extension; `additional` as `0` or `1`; the limits of the text and the answer (1 to 2,000 characters each, counted in Unicode characters after trimming, no null character) and of the file (1 to 1,000 questions, at most 2 MB). It also says what the import does with the file, so the assistant writes the right content: the import replaces every question of the subject, so a file holds the whole bank, and to add questions to a bank the administrator exports it and gives the assistant the exported file to extend; the rows become the bank in their order, oldest first, which the study materials (milestone 12.10) follow; texts are shown exactly as written and never translated, so they are plain text in the language of the competition, without Markdown and without a number of their own, since the files number the questions, and never start with `=`, which a spreadsheet reads as a formula; the reference answer is what a team's answer is graded against (milestone 12.4), so it is complete enough to grade by; and the additional questions (milestone 12.9) are kept back from participants until a competition that holds them starts. The section ends with a complete example file of a few Ukrainian questions, one of them additional, with a comma, a quote and a line break inside a field, and a short checklist the assistant goes through before handing the file over. The README section is the one description of the format for anyone outside the application; it agrees with this milestone and the specification, and a test imports its example file, so the README cannot drift from the import.
    _Import questions:_ opens a page of its own titled "Import questions: <subject>", which says how many questions the subject has now and warns, as a destructive confirmation, that importing deletes all of them and replaces them with the questions of the file, that this cannot be undone, and to export the current questions first to keep them. It holds a file field that accepts `.csv` and the destructive button **Replace questions**, with **Cancel** back to the tab. The page is a destructive confirmation (UI guidelines, "Confirmations") with a form inside: the confirmation component gets optional form content, placed between the consequence and the buttons, and sends a file when that content holds one. The file field is a new form component: a native file input with its label, a hint ("A CSV file in the format described in the README, at most 2 MB") and its error. After an error it is empty again, since a browser never refills a file field, and the administrator chooses the file again. The errors of the file are shown in the error summary of milestone 10, in a new variant: errors not linked to a field, each with its row, such as "Row 7: Enter the reference answer.", at most 20, then the line "And 5 more errors."; an error of the whole file ("Save the file as CSV UTF-8") has no row. These three patterns (the confirmation with a form, the file field and the row errors of the error summary) are added to the component library, the component gallery and the UI guidelines first, and only then used on the page (constitution Principle II). After a successful import the administrator returns to the **Subjects** tab, where the **Questions** column shows the new number.
    _Checking the file:_ the whole file is checked before anything changes, and the import is all or nothing: if any check fails, the page shows again with every error found, each with its row number in the file as a spreadsheet shows it (the header is row 1, and skipped empty rows are counted, so the number is the one the administrator sees in the spreadsheet), at most the first 20 errors with a line saying how many more there are, and the subject's questions stay exactly as they were. The limit counts errors, not rows: a row with an empty text and an empty answer gives two errors. Errors are listed in row order, and within a row in column order. A quote that is opened and never closed is refused with "A quote opened in row N is never closed." at the row where it opens, and the check stops there, since the rest of the file cannot be split into rows. The file is refused when no file is chosen, when it is larger than 2 MB, when it is not UTF-8 ("Save the file as CSV UTF-8"), when the header row is missing or wrong, when it holds no question, or when it holds more than 1,000 questions. A row is refused when it has a different number of fields than the header, when `additional` is anything but `0`, `1` or empty, or when its question text or reference answer breaks the rules of the add form (milestone 11): each is cleaned and checked by the same rules (line endings, a null character, trimming, 1 to 2,000 characters), with the same messages. Two rows with the same text are allowed, as on the add form.
    _Replacing the questions:_ in one transaction, every question of the subject that is not yet deleted is deleted the way a teacher deletes it (milestone 11), a soft deletion that keeps it stored, so the competitions that hold it keep their copies and results (milestones 12.2, 12.8) and the subject, which still has deleted questions, can still not be deleted (milestone 6). Then every row of the file becomes a new question of the subject, in the order of the file, so the first row is the oldest; each gets a new number of the bank, the numbers of the deleted questions are never reused, and each takes the additional mark of its row. A competition that holds a deleted question keeps it, with the "Deleted from the question bank" mark of milestone 12.2, even an **Upcoming** one, whose teacher with **Can manage competitions** removes it and adds a new one if they wish; nothing else in a competition changes. The study materials of milestone 12.10 show the new questions at their next download, as for any other change of the bank. While an import replaces the questions, the question bank is locked for writing in the same transaction: every other change of the bank, of any subject (a teacher adding, editing, deleting or marking a question, or another import), waits until the import ends, and reading the bank is not blocked. So a question a teacher adds at the same moment is either added before the import and replaced with the rest, or added after it, never left beside the imported ones, and a teacher editing a question of the subject while it is replaced gets the page they would get for any deleted question (milestone 11). SQLite locks the database for any write transaction; on PostgreSQL the lock is explicit, a database-specific feature justified in the plan (constitution Principle VII). A subject that has had questions can never be deleted again, even after a mistaken import; it can still be deactivated. The import logs one line with the subject id and the numbers of deleted and added questions, never a question text.
    _Test:_ Only administrators see **Export questions** and **Import questions** and the **Questions** column, and teachers and students get "access denied" from both addresses; an unknown subject answers 404 for both. A row's actions are in its actions menu in the order above, and **Deactivate** and **Activate** act only through their confirmation pages. The export is offered as a link marked as a download, for an active and a deactivated subject, and gives every listed question of the subject, oldest first, additional ones with `1`, without deleted questions, with Cyrillic text and line breaks, UTF-8 with a byte-order mark, named as above; an empty subject gives the header row only. Importing an exported file unchanged gives a bank with the same texts, answers, marks and order under new numbers. An import deletes every listed question of the subject and adds the file's in its order, leaves other subjects' questions alone, keeps the deleted questions stored and the competitions holding them unchanged, and the subject still cannot be deleted. On PostgreSQL, a teacher's change of the bank sent while an import runs waits until the import ends. A file saved by a spreadsheet with semicolons, without the byte-order mark or with `\r\n` line endings is accepted, and so are empty rows (blank or `;;`), which are skipped but counted in the row numbers, spaces around an `additional` value, and a quote inside an unquoted field. A text starting with `=` is exported and imported back unchanged. A quote that is never closed is refused at the row where it opens. A row with both an empty text and an empty answer gives two errors. A file with no file chosen, too large, not UTF-8, a wrong header, no questions, more than 1,000 questions, a row of the wrong width, a wrong `additional` value, or an empty or too long text or answer is refused with its row number, and nothing changes in the bank; a file with 25 bad rows lists 20 errors and says 5 more. The README has the section "Question bank CSV files", and its example file, taken from the README by the test, imports without an error and gives its questions in order, with the additional one marked. The confirmation with a form, the file field and the row errors of the error summary are in the component gallery. After a refused file the file field is empty. Its look and the **Import questions** page in both languages and both themes are checked by the product owner from screenshots, as a manual step of the implementation.

13. **Answer evaluation via LLM.** Each team's answer receives a proposed grade, a whole number from 0 to 10 on the scale of the manual grade (milestone 12.4), that takes level of detail into account; the evaluation is saved to the database. The proposed grade is the foundation of the answer's grade, and a teacher with **Can manage competitions** can still adjust it by hand until the competition's results are published (milestone 12.8); the stored grade, proposed or adjusted, is the one that counts, and a team's score stays the sum of its grades. The evaluation never runs on a competition whose results are published and never changes its grades. Answers and reference answers are mostly in Ukrainian (milestone 12.5), and the evaluation is designed and tested on Ukrainian answers; any text it shows to teachers is in the language of the answers. The details are refined after milestone 12.4 is shown to the product owner.
    _Test:_ evaluation returns a structured result and it is persisted; a proposed grade can be adjusted by hand, and the team's score follows the adjusted grade; a competition whose results are published is not evaluated and its grades do not change.

14. **Best answer selection + results.** For each question, the best answer is selected from the teams' answers and saved. Each team's score, the sum of its stored grades (milestone 12.4), and its place, worked out from the scores, are calculated and displayed. The winner is the team with the highest score. The details are refined after milestone 12.4 is shown to the product owner.
    _Only published results:_ the results (each team's score and place, and the winner) are shown only for a **Published** competition (milestone 12.8). Until its results are published its grades may still change, so a **Finished** competition shows no results yet; its scores are seen only on the **Grades** page, by teachers with **Can manage competitions** (milestone 12.4), and on the read-only **Answers** pages, by the teachers of each team's institution. Once a competition is published its grades never change, so the scores and places shown always agree with its stored results files, and like the grades, its best answers no longer change either. The results of a published competition offer **Download results** to teachers (milestone 12.8); the stored files stay the official confirmation of the results.
    _Kept for the record:_ a completed competition keeps showing every one of its questions and its subject, even after the question was deleted from the question bank or the subject was deactivated (milestone 11), and every one of its teams with its answers, even after the team was deleted (milestone 11.1). Its stored results files keep the results as they were at publishing.
    _Who sees results:_ the published results are open information (milestone 12.8): every teacher, with Read or Write access (milestone 8) and with or without **Can manage competitions**, sees the score and place of every team of a **Published** competition, whatever its institution. A team's answers and its grades per question stay scoped: a teacher sees them read-only for the teams of their own institutions (milestone 12.4), and a teacher with **Can manage competitions** sees the grades of every team of a competition on its **Grades** page, to grade them (milestone 12.4).
